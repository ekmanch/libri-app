# Libri: settled design

Everything here was decided during the design phase. Screen names ("Library › Filter sheet") refer to the screens and their named states in `design/canvas.html`; a screen name alone means the whole screen. Items under "Open" in section 10 are not decided yet.

## 1. What the app is

- A phone-first Flutter app for Android (Samsung Galaxy S25). A Linux desktop build may follow.
- Portrait only: the Android manifest locks the activity and `main()` sets the preferred orientation, so no screen needs a landscape layout.
- Front end for the private repo `ekmanch/library`. `library.json` in that repo is the source of truth.
- The app adds, edits and deletes entries from day one, and writes the changes back to the repo.
- Six types: Movies, Shows, Books, Games, Music, Anime. The word in the UI is "Type".

## 2. Words and rules used everywhere

| Thing | Decision |
|---|---|
| Statuses | Want, In progress, Done, DNF. Movies and music have no In progress: a movie or an album is watched or heard in one sitting, so it goes straight from Want to Done. Music has no DNF either: a song is minutes long, so "did not finish" only makes sense for long-form media. Movies keep DNF. Shows, books, games and anime have all four. |
| Rating | 1 to 10, optional, picked in a sheet (Library › Rating picker), never a slider |
| Favorite | The `#favorite` tag stays, alongside the rating |
| Row content | Title plus an italic subtitle. Subtitle is the year; for books and music it is creator and year; for a song it is album and year; for a show with more than one season the years it ran, from its first season out to its last one out, and the count of all its seasons ("2025–2026 · 2 seasons", "1990 · 2 seasons" when they all came out in one year, "2026 · 2 seasons" with Season 2 still coming). No tags in rows. A show with a new season that is out gets an outlined "New season" badge in the accent ink after the subtitle (Library › Home). |
| Not released yet | Subtitle reads "Not released yet" |
| Note vs About | Note is my own text. About is the description fetched from a lookup. |
| Delete | Lives in the details sheet. Every destructive button (Delete, Delete plan, Delete everywhere, Disconnect, Replace…) has no fill, the grey button outline and red text; never a red fill, never next to an accent button. |
| Spelling | American, sentence case |
| Type order | Wherever entries are grouped by type (home, filtered results, plan checklists, the pool), the groups follow the global order: Movies, Shows, Books, Games, Music, Anime. A mockup showing another order is incidental. The timeline groups by time instead and uses this order inside each bucket. |

## 3. Navigation

- Floating pill at the bottom with two destinations: **Library** and **Plans**. A round "+" button sits to its right.
- Settings is reached with a gear icon on top-level screens.
- Inside a plan, "⋮" opens the plan menu (Plans › Plan menu).
- No tinted header bars.
- Tapping the current tab's item in the pill returns to that tab's overview one step at a time. A screen above the overview is popped first (Plans to its overview, Plans › Plans overview; Library to the home). On the Library home the next tap scrolls back to the top, so the title, the search box and the chips are in view again, and the tap after that returns to All: the filter and the type cleared, the status segment reset, the chip row slid back so All is in view (Library › Home). Settled 2026-10-16, after the first week on the phone.
- Each tab keeps its own stack. A shortcut to something another tab owns switches to that tab with that tab's overview underneath, so back lands there: "Active plan" on the home opens the plan on Plans. Shared detail views (entry details, an artist, an album) stay in the tab they were opened from: an entry opened from a plan stays in Plans, one opened from the library stays in Library. Full-screen forms, search and Settings open above both tabs.
- A pushed screen opens with Flutter's Android transition (it fades and slides in a little from the right; the back gesture peeks at the screen below), shortened from 450 ms to 200 ms so a tap feels quick. A row that opens a screen has no press highlight: the screen opening is the feedback (tag group rows, season rows). Settled 2026-10-19.

Plan rules:

- A plan has a name, an optional year, linked tags (multi-select from existing tags; an entry matches if it has any of them), a Timeline switch and a Seasonal theme switch. Both switches default to the values in Settings.
- Plan states, from the Plans › Edit plan note: Not started, Active, Finished.
  - Active: the Started switch is on, or the plan's first date has come. When the first date arrives the app turns Started on, so the switch always shows the real state. A plan never goes inactive on its own after its end date.
  - Finished, automatically: the plan has at least one entry and every entry is done or removed from the plan. An empty plan is never Finished. Finishing shows a short "Halloween 2026 finished" message with Undo, which unticks the last item.
  - Ticking an item also turns Started on, so unticking an entry or adding a new one to a Finished plan makes it Active again.
  - Removing an entry from a plan never removes it from the library. There is no separate close action.
  - The Started help text: on, "Active. Stays active after its end date until every entry is done or removed."; off, "Not started. Turns on by itself when the plan's first date comes."
- The plan header is the same on the checklist, the timeline and the "All <plan>" tab (Plans › Halloween touches, Christmas touches and their long plan name states): row 1 is the name (one line, 32, 28 then 24 px, then an ellipsis) and the ⋮ menu; row 2 is the year chip, the progress bar and "X of Y done". The year chip is never next to the title, and its menu opens right below it.
- "When" on a plan item is empty, a single day, or a period.
- Rewatch or re-read is detected automatically: an entry that is already Done when it is added to a plan is a repeat. Its row says Rewatch for movies, shows and anime, Reread for books, Replay for games and Relisten for music.
- Removing an entry from a plan offers "Remove from this plan" or "Delete everywhere" (Plans › Remove from plan, or everywhere).
- Seasonal theme: an illustrated 88 px strip at the top, an icon by the title, a themed progress marker and a seasonal accent color. Only inside that plan.

### First run

The First run screen has these states: Splash, Welcome ("Welcome to Libri"), Connect GitHub, Pick a branch, Library file: new or existing ("Your library file: new or existing"), Create a new library, Pick an existing file and All set. They replace the old first-time setup mockup. ## 5. Music

- Music is tracked at album level. A single song can also be an entry (`kind: "song"`).
- **Library › Music: artists, text view** has three tabs: Artists (default), Albums, Songs. Albums and Songs are flat lists.
- Every artist with at least one album or song appears in the same list. The row says what is saved: "7 albums", "2 albums, 3 songs", "1 song".
- Sorting ignores a leading "The" but displays the full name. Names starting with a digit go under "#".
- "Various Artists" (today's "Compilations") is pinned at the very end, after Z.
- An alphabet rail on the right jumps to a letter, under the same rule as every long list (the Library › Long list, Long grid and Filtered results states: three screens tall, two sections or more). The text and list views get letter headers; the grid has none and uses the fast-scroll thumb with its letter bubble instead.
- **One artist** shows an Albums section, then a Songs section.
- **A song entry** has an optional `album` text field, normally filled in by the lookup, shown as its subtitle ("Bad Music for Bad People · 1981").

### Track ratings (Music › Album with track ratings)

- Opening an album writes nothing. The track list is fetched from the lookup and cached on the device.
- Rating a track adds it to the album entry's `tracks` list. Clearing the rating removes it. Only rated tracks are stored.
- A rated track is not a song entry. It has a title, a lookup ID and a rating, nothing else.
- The Songs tab lists song entries and rated tracks together, both with the subtitle "Artist · Year": the year the song, or the album it is on, came out. Tapping a rated track opens its album. Plan rows, which also mix artists, use the same "Artist · Year" for songs. On an artist's own screen a song keeps "Album · Year".
- Offline with no cached track list: show the rated tracks and a line saying the full list needs a connection.

### Replace dialogs

- **Add / edit entry › Saving an album: songs already saved**, saving an album when song entries from it exist: Keep both / Replace songs with album / Cancel. Replacing moves the songs' ratings into the album's `tracks`.
- **Add / edit entry › Saving a song: album already saved**, saving a song when its album exists: Keep both / Replace album with this song / Cancel. The dialog says what would be lost.
- A match needs the same artist and the same album (by lookup ID when available, otherwise by title). No album info on the song means no dialog. Nothing is ever merged without asking.

## 7. Tags

- Tags belong to categories, in this order: Genres (movies, shows, books and anime), Game genres, Music genres, Mood (all types), Season, Authors, Directors (also showrunners and anime directors), Actors (movies and shows; canvas revision of 2026-10-18), Studios (game and animation studios), Animation style, Other. Every tag belongs to one category, picked when it is created (Add / edit entry › New tag: confirm and pick a category), except people and genres, which can be in several categories of their kind since 2026-10-21 (see below). A new tag starts on Game genres for a game, Music genres for music and Genres otherwise. Authors, Directors and Studios replaced the one Creators group on 2026-10-16 (canvas revision Library › Filter sheet; Library › Filter: all Authors; Add / edit entry › New tag: confirm and pick a category; Add / edit entry › Tag picker); the data repo's eleven creator tags were sorted by hand (seven authors, four directors) without renaming any.
- A creator tag's group is the person's or studio's main role, not the entry's type: #stephen-king is in Authors even on a movie.
- Within every group, tags are listed A to Z by the tag itself, so people sort by first name (#ray-bradbury under R, #stephen-king under S), and a leading "the" is ignored. The order in `library.json` is not touched: a new tag is still appended, and the sort is a display rule (`lib/features/library/tag_sort.dart`).
- A group of more than 24 tags (typically a creator group) is one row, "Authors · 64 authors · 1 selected ›", that opens the full-screen list Library › Filter: all Authors; shorter groups stay inline chips. While a search is typed, a long group shows its matches inline like any other group. The same row and screen serve the filter sheet (Library › Filter sheet), the tag picker (Add / edit entry › Tag picker) and the plan's linked hashtags (Plans › Edit plan; Plans › New plan).
- The creator field suggests its tag (Add / edit entry, `lib/features/entry/creator_tags.dart`): the author of a book (Authors), the director or showrunner of a movie or show (Directors), the studio of a game or an anime (Studios), as a slug of the name ("Ursula K. Le Guin" is #ursula-k-le-guin, "Jo Nesbø" is #jo-nesbo); several names ("Stephen King & Peter Straub") give several tags. Music suggests nothing, since an artist is not a tag. A tag the library has goes on at once like a matched genre when the entry comes from a lookup; otherwise, and for a tag that does not exist yet, it is offered under the field as a dashed chip (marked "new" when new), and creating it opens Add / edit entry › New tag: confirm and pick a category on its creator group. The suggestion follows the field as it is edited, but waits until the field is left, so a half-typed name is never offered. Editing an existing entry never adds a tag by itself.
- Studios whose name is how their films are known get the studio's tag instead of the director's: a Pixar film suggests #pixar, not the director; likewise #disney (Walt Disney Animation Studios, Walt Disney Productions, DisneyToon), #dreamworks (DreamWorks Animation) and #studio-ghibli. The studio comes from the lookup's production companies (TMDB), studios (AniList) or developers (IGDB), checked in that order so a Pixar film that also lists Walt Disney Pictures is #pixar; "Walt Disney Pictures" alone is a live-action film and keeps its director. Typed by hand, "Hayao Miyazaki" and "Isao Takahata" still mean #studio-ghibli. The table is `brandStudios` and `houseDirectors` in `creator_tags.dart`.
- Filter sheet (Library › Filter sheet): a category group shows only when at least one of its tags is used by an entry of the chosen types. This comes from the library, not a fixed mapping, so Games shows Genres too once a game is tagged #crime, and Studios shows with Movies chosen once a movie carries #pixar. With no type chosen, every group shows. A chosen tag whose group goes away is dropped.
- Tag picker (Add / edit entry › Tag picker): Game genres only for games, Music genres only for music and Actors only for movies and shows, unless you search; a search finds any tag. Every group starts folded each time the picker opens, except the groups that already hold a tag on this entry and "New on this entry", which is always open; a folded row shows "n selected"; typing in the search shows the matches from every group, folded or not (canvas revision of 2026-10-18). The picker opens without the keyboard, since a tag is usually tapped rather than typed; the search box takes the keyboard on a tap. Plan hashtags (Plans › Edit plan; Plans › New plan) list every category.
- Add / edit entry suggests actor tags for a movie or show from the lookup's billed cast: the top three, as slugs (#kate-siegel) in the Actors group, marked "new" like any other suggestion, never more than three, and outside the six genre suggestions. An actor the library already has goes on at once, matched by exact slug only (never the fuzzy match genres get). The lookup carries the cast on `LookupMatch.cast`; TMDB fills it from the credits of a movie or a show.
- A new tag can only be created from the entry form's tag picker, so every tag is used by at least one entry. A tag that no entry uses disappears.
- A tag is lowercase ASCII letters, digits and hyphens, plus a dot between two digits (#2.5d, owner's choice of 2026-10-19, since #2-5d reads as a range). Whatever is typed or suggested is cleaned to that: diacritics folded, apostrophes dropped, any other run of characters one hyphen (`normalizeTag` in `lib/features/entry/similar_tags.dart`). The data repo's `library.py` checks the same rule.
- Before creating a tag, check for similar existing ones: same when hyphens and dots are ignored; one letter different (two for tags of six letters or more); two neighboring letters swapped; singular versus plural. If one is found, show Add / edit entry › New tag: a similar one exists.
- Creating a tag always goes through Add / edit entry › New tag: confirm and pick a category to confirm it and pick its category.
- Tags in more than one category (canvas revision of 2026-10-21; Add / edit entry › Tag in two categories: entry form, Tag in two categories: tag picker, New tag; Library › Entry details: tag in two categories): a person can be in Authors, Directors and Actors, a genre in Genres, Game genres and Music genres, never across kinds (#horror cannot become an actor; Mood, Season, Studios, Animation style and Other stay one category each). The categories belong to the tag: the `tags` map keeps one list per category, and such a tag is listed in each (`Library.categoriesOf`). On each entry the tag is ticked per category: Million Dollar Baby has #clint-eastwood under Directors and Actors, The Good, the Bad and the Ugly only under Actors (`tag_categories`, section 8).
- Tag picker: a tag in several categories appears in each of its groups and is ticked independently; the first category ticked puts the tag on the entry, the last unticked takes it off. A long press on a tag (a chip, or a row in Library › Filter: all Authors) opens its categories: the categories of its kind as chips, at least one picked, with where it is used ("Used on 6 entries: as an actor on 4, as a director on 2."). Searching for a person or genre that another category of the same kind has shows the tag under each category of the kind that has no match of its own, with "Not an actor tag yet" and an "Add to Actors" chip: it adds the category and ticks it on this entry. New tag: one or more categories of the same kind; tapping a category of another kind starts over with it; Create tag needs at least one. Category changes made in the picker are applied with `SetTagCategories` right before the entry is saved, like new tags with `AddTag`; a cancelled form changes nothing.
- When a category is taken away from a tag, every entry keeps the categories it had that remain; an entry that had the tag only in the category taken away loses the tag (the usage line in the dialog says how many), and a tag left on no entry is pruned as usual. A tag back to one category has its picks removed everywhere.
- Where a tag in several categories shows as text, its category follows the name as a role: the form's Tags box shows one chip per category ("#clint-eastwood · Director", "#clint-eastwood · Actor"), details merge them ("#clint-eastwood · Director, Actor"), an active filter chip reads "#clint-eastwood · Director", and Library › Search lists one row per category ("#clint-eastwood · Actor · 2 entries"), each opening the filter on that category. Under a group heading (tag picker, filter sheet) no role is shown. One-category tags look as before (`tagLabel`, `TagCategory.roleLabel`).
- Filters match per category: Directors › #clint-eastwood matches only entries where he is ticked as a director; both Directors and Actors ticked matches entries where he is both (`LibraryFilter.tagCategories`). The lookup ticks per category: the creator field's director and the top-billed cast each tick their role, so a director who also stars is ticked as both; a person the library has only under another people category is offered under the field with the new role ("#clint-eastwood · Actor", no "new" badge) and a tap adds the category and ticks it.

## 8. Data: `library.json` version 2

```json
{
  "version": 2,
  "tags": { "Genres": ["horror", "scifi"], "Mood": ["lighthearted"] },
  "entries": [
    {
      "id": "movies/the-mummy-1999",
      "type": "movies",
      "title": "The Mummy",
      "released": "1999",
      "creator": "Stephen Sommers",
      "status": "done",
      "rating": 9,
      "tags": ["adventure", "lighthearted", "halloween"],
      "note": "",
      "description": "",
      "seasonal_only": false,
      "added": "2026-10-01",
      "source": null
    }
  ],
  "plans": [
    {
      "id": "halloween-2026",
      "name": "Halloween",
      "year": 2026,
      "tags": ["halloween"],
      "timeline": true,
      "seasonal_theme": true,
      "started": true,
      "items": [
        { "entry": "movies/the-mummy-1999", "done": false, "repeat": true, "note": "", "when": { "from": "2026-10-19", "to": "2026-10-31" } }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

Field rules:

- `id`: `<type>/<title>-<year>` for every type but music (`movies/the-mummy-1999`, `books/dracula-1897`), `music/<title>-<artist>` for music (`music/dirt-alice-in-chains`). One scheme for the app and the data: the phase 3 migration assigned every existing id with the app's `EntryId.generate`, so nothing in `ekmanch/library` follows an older rule.
- `type`: `movies`, `shows`, `books`, `games`, `music`, `anime`.
- `released` is text with flexible precision: `"1999"`, `"2026-12"` or `"2026-12-25"`. There is no separate `year`; the app derives the year and whether the title is out yet.
- `creator` is one field; the app labels it Director, Author, Artist and so on by type.
- `status`: `want`, `in_progress`, `done`, `dnf`.
- `rating`: 1 to 10 or `null`.
- `seasonal_only`: true for titles only wanted in season; hidden from the main lists unless "Include seasonal-only" is on.
- `tag_categories` (canvas revision of 2026-10-21, Add / edit entry › Tag in two categories): written right after `tags`, only on an entry with a tag that the tag map lists under more than one category: `{ "clint-eastwood": ["Actors"] }`, which of the tag's categories the tag has on this entry, a non-empty list in category order, one key per such tag and no other. `tags` stays a flat list of names, so the data repo's Markdown, tag pages and PDFs keep working. The tag map itself needs no new form: a person or genre in several categories of its kind is listed in each (`"Directors": ["clint-eastwood"], "Actors": ["clint-eastwood"]`), so a file from before, with every tag in one list, is unchanged. A tag in several categories with no `tag_categories` on an entry (a file edited by hand) counts in all of them; the app writes the key whenever it saves such an entry.
- `started` on a plan: the Started switch (Plans › Edit plan). Always written, `false` by default; set by the user or by the app when the plan's first date arrives, and never cleared by the app.
- `platform` (games) uses spelled-out names, one spelling per platform: "PlayStation 5", not "PS5" (see `lib/data/models/platform_names.dart`).
- `when` on a plan item: `null`, or `from` and `to` (equal for a single day).
- Type-specific fields: `platform` (games), `formats` (books: `audiobook`, `physical`), `pages` (books: the page count, a whole number above zero, written after `formats`; canvas revision of 2026-10-19, Library › Book details (page count)), `series` (movies, books, games and anime movies, written after `pages`, see below), for music `kind` (`album` or `song`), `album` (songs, optional), `tracks` (albums, optional: `[{ "title", "id", "rating" }]`), and for anime `kind` (`series` or `movie`, see below).
- `series` (canvas revision of 2026-10-20, Library › Movie in a series): `{ "name": "The Lord of the Rings", "number": 1 }`, the series the entry is part of and its number in the source's numbering, `"number": null` when the number is not known; left out when the entry is in no series. Movies get it from TMDB's collections, games from IGDB's collections, books from Wikidata (part of the series, series ordinal) or a volume number in the title ("Berserk, Vol. 43"), anime movies by hand; Series and Number are editable in Add / edit entry. Only the name and number are stored; the full list of parts is fetched and kept on the phone. Shows and anime series never have one: they use seasons.
- `kind` on an anime (canvas revision of 2026-10-20, Show & seasons › Anime with seasons (Frieren)): `series` or `movie`, from AniList's format when the anime is picked or looked up again (TV, TV short, ONA and OVA are a series; a movie, a special or a music video is a movie) and editable in Add / edit entry; left out on an anime from before kinds, which stays like a movie until the owner picks a kind or the daily check learns it from AniList (an anime with an `anilist:` source only). An anime series carries `seasons` like a show, written last, and no date of its own; the rules for shows below apply to it in full. Written after `series`, before `seasons`.
- `seasons` (shows and anime series, written last): `[{ "season": 2, "released": "2026-10-09", "status": "in_progress" }]` in season number order. Every show has at least one season once moved (see below); "show" in the rules below means a show or an anime series. `season` is the number, unique within the show. `released` takes the three forms and the release rule of the entry's own; `"released": null` and `"status": null` are always written. `status` is `null` (shown as "Not set"), `want`, `in_progress`, `done` or `dnf`. `"new": true` follows `status` on a season the daily check added to a show that already had seasons, and is removed when the season gets a status; it shows only once the season is out. `"manual": true` follows it on a season added in the form, dated in the form, or kept when the lookup dropped it (Show & seasons › Announced season dropped); the daily check never removes or redates such a season. Both are written only while true. No per-season rating: the show keeps its one rating.
- A show has no date of its own: its `released` is `null` (the key is still written, like every common field). Sorting by release year, the decade filter, the rail and a new show's id use Season 1's year; the header and the row show the years from the first to the last season out.
- A show's `status` is set by its seasons and written on every change, so filters, sorting and the export keep working. One season: that season's status, Want while not set. More: only seasons that are out and have a status count; any In progress is In progress; else any DNF is DNF; all Done is Done; Done and Want together are In progress; all Want, or nothing set, is Want. Ticking a show in a plan marks every season that is out Done; unticking puts the seasons back as they were.
- Moving a show from before seasons ("Move show dates and statuses into seasons", one queued operation when the library loads with such a show): its own date is dropped; a show with no seasons and no TMDB link gets Season 1 with that date and its status, marked manual; a show with seasons none of which has a status gets the old status carried over: Want or Done to every season out, In progress or DNF to the latest season out with the earlier ones Done (one season takes it whether out or not). A show linked to TMDB with no seasons yet keeps its date until the daily check fills it in, and the carry-over happens then.
- `source`: the lookup id the entry was found by (to re-fetch covers, track lists, seasons and series), for example `"tmdb:movie:694"`, or `null`. Always written, right after `added` and before the type-specific fields; `null` for every migrated entry and for entries added by hand. The data repo's validator only checks that it is text or `null`, not its format. The app writes `tmdb:movie:<id>` and `tmdb:tv:<id>` (movies, shows), `anilist:<id>`, `openlibrary:<work id>` (never an edition), `itunes:<track id>` (a book Open Library does not have, from Apple Books), `googlebooks:<volume id>` (only on books added on 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-20, while Google Books was a search source), `igdb:<id>`, `deezer:album:<id>` and `deezer:track:<id>`; a rated track's `id` is `deezer:track:<id>`. Only a pick in Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match or "Look up again" in Add / edit entry sets it; covers and track lists for entries without one are matched on the device and never written back. Changing an entry's type clears it; editing the title never does.

Not in the file: covers, track lists, season episodes, the parts of a series, an anime's TMDB match, view choices, theme, sort order. Those are caches or device preferences.

Rules settled during phase 0:

- A partial `released` counts as released only after its period ends: `"2026-12-25"` on that day, `"2026-12"` from 2027-01-01, `"2027"` from 2028-01-01. A game "coming in 2027" stays "Not released yet" all year. No `released` at all counts as released.
- The file is written in one canonical form so that a save is a minimal git diff: two-space indent, one trailing newline, keys in the order of the sample above, entries and plans never reordered by an edit. Common entry fields are always written, including `"rating": null`; the type-specific fields are omitted when absent. Decoding and re-encoding a canonical file gives back the same bytes.
- Entry ids are slugs frozen at creation: lowercase, apostrophes dropped (`salems-lot`, not `salem-s-lot`), diacritics folded to ASCII, runs of other characters become one hyphen; `<type>/<title>-<year>` for every type but music (books too, never the author), or `music/<title>-<artist>`. A collision gets `-2`, `-3`, for every type (`EntryId.generate`, covered by `test/data/models/entry_id_test.dart`). Editing a title never changes the id, so plan items keep pointing at the right entry.
- A tag disappears from `tags` only when no entry **and no plan** uses it, so a new plan tagged `halloween` keeps the tag alive before any entry has it.

Migration from version 1 (`catalog.<type>` plus `seasonal.<season>.<type>.{plans, coming_later, favorites}`; 659 entries, 425 of them music):

- `dropped` becomes `dnf`; `year` becomes `released`.
- Seasonal favorites merge into the entries.
- Plan and "Coming later" rows become `want` entries tagged with their season, plus plan items.
- The artist "Compilations" becomes "Various Artists".
- The Markdown files and PDFs become output generated from the JSON.

## 9. Sync with GitHub

- Show cached data immediately, then check for changes.
- Check on open and resume, on pull-to-refresh, on tab switch if more than 5 minutes have passed, and always right before saving.
- Use conditional requests so an unchanged file costs almost nothing. Always request the raw format, which supports files up to 100 MB.
- The UI updates at once; saving happens in the background.
- Edits made within 2 to 3 seconds are bundled into one commit with a readable message, such as "Mark The Mummy as done".
- On a conflict, fetch the new file and re-apply the pending operations. Operations are small ("set status of X"), so this is safe.
- Offline edits are queued and sent when the connection returns.
- All storage goes through one interface, so the file can later be split per type without touching the UI.

## 10a. Decisions made while building

- Section headers are uppercase, except decades ("1990s", not "1990S").

## 10b. Sync, as built in phase 4

- Files on the device, in the `libri` folder: `library.json` (the cache of the file on GitHub, in the canonical form), `pending.json` (the operations not yet on GitHub, each with its summary), `sync.json` (the base: which file it is about, the ETag, the blob sha of the remote file, the sha of its canonical re-encoding, the last commit, when the phone last matched GitHub and when it last asked), `connection.json` (repo, file, branch, and whether the library was kept on the phone) and `preferences.json`. The token is in the phone's secure storage only. Disconnect removes `pending.json` and the token; deleting the library in Settings › Disconnect: keep or delete removes `library.json` and `sync.json` too, keeping it leaves both for the comparison on a reconnect.
- An edit goes into `pending.json` before the cache applies it, and leaves it only after the commit that carries it succeeds. A crash between the two never leaves a change in the cache that GitHub will not get.
- Every sync is a three-way comparison against the base in `sync.json`, the file as it was when both sides last matched. The phone's copy is compared by the blob sha of its canonical encoding against the base's canonical sha (so a file formatted by hand on GitHub does not look edited), GitHub's file by its blob sha against the base's blob sha (so commits that touch other files never count). Neither changed: nothing to do. Only the phone: push, with the queue's summaries as the message, or "Save the changes made on this phone" when nothing is queued (edits made while the library was kept on the phone, or an app update that encodes differently). Only GitHub: pull. Both: a conflict (`SyncPhase.conflict`); nothing moves until the user picks a side, "Keep mine" (the phone's copy replaces GitHub's, whatever is there by then) or "Keep GitHub's" (its copy replaces the phone's and the queue is dropped). The banner (Library › Sync conflict banner on the Library, Plans › Plans with the sync conflict banner on Plans) reads "Changed here and on GitHub · Pick which copy to keep." with Choose; Settings › Library connection shows the same title with "Pick which copy to keep" and Choose in place of Sync now. Library › Conflict: keep which copy? opens from both: two muted lines ("This phone: N changes", or "edited here" when the edits were made without a queue; "GitHub: last commit Oct 13, 20:12", looked up when the conflict is found), one warning ("Whichever you keep, the other copy's changes are gone. Earlier versions stay in the repo's history."), then Keep mine (neutral fill), Keep GitHub's (destructive) and Decide later (text: closes, the banner stays, nothing syncs until a choice).
- Reconnecting a library kept on the phone to the same repo, file and branch runs that comparison, so edits made on the phone meanwhile are pushed. A base written for another file is no base. Picking another file in First run › Pick an existing file while a library is kept on the phone asks First run › Different file: which library? ("Which library do you want to keep?", with the entry counts of both): "This phone's" commits the phone's library over the chosen file ("Replace with the library from this phone") before connecting, "The one on GitHub" connects as before so GitHub's copy replaces the phone's, Cancel goes back to First run › Create a new library. Picking the kept library's own file asks nothing: the sync compares both copies to their base. "Create a new library" (First run › Create a new library) with a kept library creates the file from that library, no question asked; First run › Create a new library and First run › All set say so.
- The data repo's `library.json` re-encodes to byte-identical output through the app's codec (verified 2026-10-14 against blob `fcc0e42`: same bytes, LF endings, one trailing newline, no `\u` escapes). The repo's pre-commit hook and `scripts/generate.sh` never write `library.json`; only the app does, or careful hand edits, which the canonical sha absorbs.
- Reads use the raw media type (works to 100 MB). Its ETag is the quoted blob sha, checked live, so a save sets the ETag too and the next check is one conditional request. Writes use the Contents API PUT with the blob sha of the file being replaced, computed locally from the raw bytes (`gitBlobSha`); a 1.9 MB file was saved and read back whole in the live test, so there is no 1 MB limit to work around. The data repo's Action commits regenerated Markdown after every save; that moves the branch but not the file's blob, so nothing in the app looks at the branch head.
- A save whose answer was lost (a kill, a dropped connection) is settled before anything is sent again: `pending.json` records the operations, the blob sha and the message of the save in flight. On the next check, the remote file with that sha means it landed; the old sha means it did not; anything else is looked up in the file's last five commits by message. Nothing is saved twice.
- Checks happen on load, when the app comes to the foreground, every 5 minutes while it stays there, on a tab switch when the last check is older than that, on pull-to-refresh on the library home (Library › Home) and the plans overview (Plans › Plans overview), and always right before a save. Edits are gathered for 2.5 seconds, then sent as one commit: one operation gives its summary, several give "<first summary> and N other changes" with every summary listed in the body. A save refused for a stale sha (409 or 422) is checked again rather than retried: if GitHub's file changed since the base, that is a conflict; if not (a spurious refusal), the queue goes out on the next round. A save whose answer was lost and turns out to have landed under later changes moves the base to that save, so the later changes are pulled as GitHub's alone.
- The "Saving" banner shows only while the commit is in flight, not during the 2.5 seconds. A 403 with the rate limit used up (or a 429) is a retryable failure ("Could not save", Retry), not a token problem; 401 and other 403s are. A fine-grained token answers 404 for a repo it cannot see, so First run › Connect GitHub says "Repository not found, or the token cannot see it". First run › Connect GitHub also refuses a token that can read but not write, before the first save.
- A missing or unreadable token at start means the first run, with the last repo and file prefilled in First run › Connect GitHub. First run › Pick a branch lists up to 100 branches (one page) and asks for each head commit's date in parallel, since the branches endpoint carries none. First run › Pick an existing file opens at most 20 JSON files of at most 20 MB from the repo tree to tell libraries from other JSON, and shows each library's entry count and last commit. First run › Create a new library never overwrites an existing file.
- A 404 from GitHub during a check or a save (the file, the branch or the repo deleted, or a fine-grained token that no longer sees the repo) is `SyncPhase.notFound`, not a failed save: Retry could not help. Two more calls (the repo, then the file's commits on the branch) tell which part is gone, and the banner reads "Library not found on GitHub" with "library.json is gone from owner/repo on main." (or "The branch main is gone from owner/repo.", or "owner/repo is gone, or the token no longer sees it.") and "Nothing syncs until you reconnect."; Fix opens Settings › Library connection, whose status card says the same. Edits keep queuing on the phone and the checks go on, so a restored file or branch syncs again by itself; otherwise Disconnect keeping the library, then connect again (First run › Different file: which library? offers to put the phone's copy over another file). The dev switch `SYNC_BANNER=missing` shows the banner.
- Token expiry (Library › Token expires soon, canvas revision of 2026-10-18): GitHub answers every request made with an expiring token with a `GitHub-Authentication-Token-Expiration` header ("2025-11-10 16:20:42 +0500"). The app reads it off every successful sync answer and keeps the date in `connection.json` (`tokenExpires`), only for the token in use and only when the date is ahead (in September 2025 GitHub briefly sent the current time by mistake; a date that has passed while requests still succeed is ignored). No header, nothing stored. From seven days before the expiry, Library › Token expires soon shows once a day on the first app open that day (a start or a return to the foreground, or the moment GitHub first says so), and the day is kept in `connection.json` (`tokenWarnedOn`): "Your GitHub token expires in 6 days" (or "in 1 day", "today"), "The access token for owner/repo stops working on October 24. After that, Libri can't sync until you add a new one.", a quieter "On GitHub, regenerate the token with a new expiry date, then paste it here. Your library stays as it is.", and three stacked buttons: Replace token (neutral fill) opens the token step with the repo kept, like Settings › Library connection's; Open GitHub (outline, external-link icon) opens the fine-grained tokens page in the browser and leaves the dialog up; Later (text) dismisses it until the next day. A replaced token clears both dates; the new token's come with the next answer. After the expiry GitHub answers 401, and the sync state reads "Token expired" with "Libri can't sync until you add a new one." and a Replace token action, on the Library › Sync and errors banner and the Settings › Library connection status card, instead of "GitHub refused the token" with Fix. The dev switches `TOKEN_EXPIRES_IN=6` (Library › Token expires soon at start, that many days ahead) and `SYNC_BANNER=expired` show the states.
- The FIRST_RUN dev switch, the widget tests and the screenshot walks talk to an in-memory fake repo (`lib/dev/fake_github_api.dart`) with the canvas's three branches and three files (the files are the same on every branch). The opt-in live test (`test/data/github/github_live_test.dart`, with `LIBRI_LIVE_TOKEN` in the environment) runs against the private scratch repo `ekmanch/libri-scratch`, seeded with a copy of the real `library.json`, in a file it removes at the end. `ekmanch/library` is never touched from the tests.

## 10c. Lookups and images, as built in phase 5

- Keys: copy `secrets.example.json` to `secrets.json` (git-ignored) and run with `flutter run --dart-define-from-file=secrets.json`. `TMDB_READ_TOKEN`, `IGDB_CLIENT_ID` and `IGDB_CLIENT_SECRET` are needed for their types; `GOOGLE_BOOKS_KEY` is optional and only used to read a stored `googlebooks:` id; Apple Books needs none. A missing key leaves that provider "not set up": nothing is asked and nothing crashes. The IGDB token Twitch issues is kept in `igdb_token.json` under the cache folder and fetched again a day before it expires or when IGDB refuses it. No provider is named anywhere in the UI except on About (Settings › About Libri), where every source is credited.
- Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match: typing pauses for 400 ms, then every configured source for the chosen type is asked (on All, every type in parallel, five per type, taking turns in type order; one type gets its source's ten). A thin accent line under the chips shows while a lookup is pending or in flight; refining a query keeps the rows on screen. States: `Looking up "Carrie"…` with a spinner before the first answer; `Is it one of these?` and the rows; `No matches among games.` (a type picked) or `No matches for "Carrie".` (All); `Could not reach the lookup. Check your connection and try again.` with Try again when every source was offline; `The lookup did not answer. Try again in a moment.` with Try again for anything else; `Lookups for games are not set up on this build (no API key).` or `Lookups are not set up on this build (no API keys).`; and `Some lookups could not be reached.` under the rows when only some failed, or, when those that answered found nothing, `Some lookups could not be reached, so a match may be missing. Try again in a moment.` with Try again instead of "No matches" (books count as two lookups, Open Library and Apple Books). "Fill in the details myself" stays below in every state. A complete answer is remembered for the session, so switching the type chips back and forth costs nothing.
- Picking a row fetches the full details first (a spinner replaces the chevron): TMDB's search has no director or genres, so they come with the pick; Deezer's has no release date or genres; Open Library's has no description; whichever of Open Library and Apple Books a book came from, the other fills in what it lacks with the per-field preference of section 10 (Apple's description and cover, Open Library's year, author and page count). Should that fetch fail, the form opens with what the search had. A book whose date came only from Apple Books (no Open Library year, so possibly a reprint's) shows "Year from the ebook edition. Check if it's a reprint." under Released, inside its column, in the muted color at 12.5 (Add / edit entry › Book: year from the ebook edition, canvas revision of 2026-10-20); it goes once the date is changed or cleared or the Released picker has been opened and closed. It shows only while adding, also after "Look up again" there; where the date came from is held by the match and the add form only and never stored, so Edit never shows it. The form then carries the lookup id into `source`, the released date in full where the provider has it, the description with HTML stripped, the platforms the game came out on (IGDB's "PC (Microsoft Windows)" reads as "PC"), and up to six tag suggestions. A suggested genre first goes through the alias table in `lib/features/entry/similar_tags.dart`, so "Science Fiction" and "sci-fi" become the library's `scifi` and are never offered as new tags beside it; Open Library's catalog notes and pseudo-subjects (`award:hugo_award=1960`, anything long, with punctuation or over three words) are not suggested at all.
- "Look up again" (Add / edit entry): an outline row at the top of the form whenever no match is applied (an edit of any entry, or a new entry filled in by hand) opens Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match on the entry's type and title in a pick mode without the "Fill in" footer. A pick sets `source` and replaces the lookup's fields (creator, released, description, pages, album) with the match's where it has them, keeping the form's value where the match has none; a pick means another book, or the right one this time, so the old values would be wrong (changed 2026-10-20 from filling only empty fields, on the owner's report that a re-pick left the year and description as they were). The title, note, status, tags and formats are the owner's and stay; a pick also adds the platforms as chips, offers the tag suggestions, and shows the "Details fetched online" card. Saving writes the whole entry as before, and the entry's cover is forgotten so the next look resolves through the source: after any pick, the same source as before included (the same book's cover may come from elsewhere now, as a book's does from Apple since 2026-10-20), and after a source edited by hand. Until 2026-10-20 only a changed source forgot the cover, so re-picking a linked book kept its old cover (owner's report).
- Covers and photos are shown only in the photo views, and in Library › Entry details; Music › Album with track ratings; Plans › Entry details (timeline on) and Plans › Entry details (timeline off) when that type's view is a photo view; a text view fetches nothing. `CoverImage` and `ArtistPhoto` in `lib/widgets/` draw the old placeholder (the colored box with the title, the disc with the initials) until the image is on the device and decoded, then the image, decoded at the shown width; a fresh download fades in over 200 ms. The details header's cover carries the title as its screen reader name; covers in rows are skipped by the screen reader, since the row names the title. Covers keep their slots: 2:3 posters for movies, shows, books, anime and games (IGDB's 3:4 art is cropped a little top and bottom), squares for albums and songs, discs for artists.
- Finding a cover: an entry with a `source` asks that provider for its cover (never a search). One without is matched on the device: title and year for movies, shows, books, games and anime (the same title in the same year, else a year off, else, with edition notes like "(2022 Remaster)" set aside, the same again; a different title is never taken), album title and artist for albums, and the album for a song. Books match through the same combined lookup as Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match: Open Library and Apple Books together, merged the same way; no hit while one of the two did not answer is not a miss. The result goes into `artwork.json` under the cache folder: per entry the cover URL and the id it was matched to, keyed to the title, year, creator, album and source at the time, so an edit to any of those means a new lookup. Only a real answer of no cover (no match, or a provider with no image for the id) is a miss, and it is tried again after fourteen days. A lookup that did not work out is never recorded, for any provider, covers and artist photos alike: offline (a timeout too), rate limited, not set up, a server error (5xx), a 4xx or a malformed answer are kept in memory only and asked again the next session (settled 2026-10-19). `artwork.json` is version 2 since then; the misses of a version 1 file, which could be such failures, are dropped once and looked for again, its covers kept. Artist photos come from Deezer by exact name ("Various Artists" and "Unknown artist" never ask) and are recorded by name. At most two lookups run at once, in the order the rows asked, with each provider's own gap (Apple Books three seconds, AniList two, Open Library half a second, IGDB 300 ms, Deezer 120 ms, TMDB 50 ms); a row that scrolls away before its turn is released. A photo-view scroll over fifty anime therefore takes about two minutes to fill the first time.
- Image files live under `<cache>/libri/images/<sha1 of the url>.img`, one download per URL however many rows ask at once; a failed download is not retried for six hours (thirty seconds when offline). Track lists live under `<cache>/libri/tracks/<album id>.json`. Once per session, fifteen seconds after the library first loads, the records, image files, track lists, episodes and series of entries and artists that are gone are removed; Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match thumbnails go with them at the next start.
- Music › Album with track ratings: the track list comes from the album's `source`, or from the Deezer album its cover was matched to, through `/album/{id}/tracks`, and is kept on the device; opening an album writes nothing to the library. While it loads the rated tracks show with `Loading the track list…`; offline with nothing cached, `The full track list needs a connection.`; when the source has no list (or no album could be matched), `No track list was found for this album.`; with no rated track the line starts `No tracks rated yet.`. A stored rating is matched to a listed track by id when one matches, else by title with case, punctuation and edition notes set aside, so the ratings written under earlier ids (the fixture's `mb:` ones) still show against a Deezer list. - Seasons (settled 2026-10-18 with the canvas revisions of Show & seasons › Show with seasons (Haunted Hotel) to Add / edit entry › Edit a show): a show picked in Add / edit entry › New entry: pick a match, or linked with "Look up again" while its seasons are all manual, gets TMDB's seasons from the details call (`/tv/{id}`): every numbered season with a date (an announced one too, shown "Coming 2027"), with no status; season 0 (specials) and seasons TMDB lists with no date yet are left out. The daily check runs on the first app open of the day and on each return to the foreground until it has run that day; there is no fixed time, and nothing runs while Libri is closed. It keeps `season_check.json` under the cache folder, never in `library.json`: the day it last ran and the seasons it found missing on that run. It asks TMDB (`/tv/{id}`, one request per show, through the provider's gap) about every show with a `tmdb:tv:` source that is not DNF, and once about any show without seasons whatever its status. It adds dated seasons numbered above the show's highest (so a removed season does not come back unless it was the last), marked New on a show that had seasons and quietly on one that had none (its date dropped, its status carried over); updates the date of a season not out yet that Libri added when TMDB dates it differently ("Update the season dates of X"); and writes a status the seasons no longer give, when a season has come out ("Update the status of X from its seasons"). A season not out yet that Libri added, missing from TMDB or on a show TMDB marks Canceled on two checks in a row, is removed quietly when Not set ("Remove season 2 of Carrie") and asked about in Show & seasons › Announced season dropped when it has a status; manual seasons and seasons out are never touched. Offline, rate limited or a server error (5xx) for any show, the day is not recorded and the next open tries again (a 404 or a bad answer for a show is nothing learned and does not hold the day back); a build without the TMDB key asks nothing and only writes drifted statuses. - Series (canvas revision of 2026-10-20, Library › Movie in a series; Book in a long series; All volumes in a series): a pick fills `series` from the lookup. TMDB: a movie's `belongs_to_collection`, the collection read for the parts in date order ("The Lord of the Rings Collection" is the series "The Lord of the Rings"). IGDB: the game's `collections` (the single `collection` field is retired and empty; fixed 2026-10-21) with their games, keeping main games and remakes that are no edition of another (`game_type` 0 and 8, `version_parent` null), in date order; a game in several collections ("Donkey Kong Country" is in "Donkey Kong Country" and "Donkey Kong") takes the one its title starts with, the longest name first, else the first listed. Books: Wikidata first, by the title through the entity search (case does not matter there, and Open Library titles are often in sentence case) and one SPARQL query for the candidates' "part of the series" (P179) with the series ordinal (P1545), the author (P50, a surname in common with the entry's creator tells the book from a film of the same name) and every part of the series with its ordinal and date (P577), asked again with the title cut before a parenthesis, a colon or ", Part" when the full title names nothing (`WikidataSeries.coreTitle`: Open Library has "Tai-Pan (Asian Saga"); else a volume number in the title ("Berserk, Vol. 43", "Volume 43", "#43", `volumeOf`); Wikidata not answering leaves the series to the title. A stored list shows at once and is fetched again after seven days, since a series grows. Rows match the library's entries to the parts by lookup id, else by number, else by title; entries the lookup does not list are placed by their number. Anime movies and series typed by hand have no fetchable list, so their rows are the library's own entries. Wikidata is credited on Settings › About Libri, last.
